## Linguistic and educational references

1. **OJAD development team / University of Tokyo.** *OJAD — Online Japanese Accent Dictionary.* [English home](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/eng/pages/home), [word search](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/search), [verb suffix search](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/kouzokugo), [usage conditions and acknowledgments](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/pages/notes). Used for individual form/reading/accent comparisons and as an educational resource. The usage page describes the project as a NINJAL collaborative-project outcome led by Nobuaki Minematsu at the University of Tokyo. It limits use to educational/academic research activities and asks users to refrain from use in corporate R&D for profit. Public accessibility is not an open-data license; no permission to integrate or redistribute its database/audio is assumed.

2. **Minematsu, Nobuaki; Nakamura, Ibuki; Suzuki, Masayuki; Hirano, Hiroko; Nakagawa, Chieko; Nakamura, Noriko; Tagawa, Yukinori; Hirose, Keikichi; and Hashimoto, Hiroya. 2017.** “Development and Evaluation of Online Infrastructure to Aid Teaching and Learning of Japanese Prosody.” *IEICE Transactions on Information and Systems* E100.D(4): 662–669. [DOI and publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1587/transinf.2016AWI0007). Publisher metadata and abstract verified. Used to distinguish OJAD's conjugation, auxiliary-expression, and sentence-prosody tasks. Its reported evaluations belong to that system, not MoraMaro.

3. **Kubozono, Haruo. 2014.** “Japanese Pitch Accent in a Typological Perspective.” *Proceedings of the 4th International Symposium on Tonal Aspects of Languages (TAL 2014)*, Nijmegen, 195–198. [ISCA archive PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/tal_2014/kubozono14_tal.pdf). Read for Tokyo lexical contrast, examples (2)–(3), and the typological comparison of accent systems. The four noun patterns in the pitch chapter are attributed to those examples.

4. **Schökler, Thomas. 2019.** “Deriving Accent from Structure: The Case of Japanese Verbs.” ICPP 2019, poster-session abstract [1-22], University of Vienna affiliation. [Supplied NINJAL-hosted PDF](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/past-projects/crosslinguistic-studies/prosody/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2019/10/1-22_ICPP2019_Schoekler.pdf); [official conference program](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/past-projects/crosslinguistic-studies/prosody/index6f6d.html). Author/title/session verified in the program. The one-page PDF was detected but yielded no readable text, and the screenshot fetch failed. It is a relevant structural research lead, not substantively verified evidence for a rule or example in this foundation. No journal publication or page range is invented.

5. **Kawahara, Shigeto. 2015.** “The Phonology of Japanese Accent.” In Haruo Kubozono (ed.), *Handbook of Japanese Phonetics and Phonology*, 445–492. De Gruyter Mouton. [Author-hosted chapter](https://shigetokawahara.github.io/pdf/HandbookAccentPublished.pdf), [NINJAL bibliographic record](https://bibdb.ninjal.ac.jp/bunken/ja/article/200000203196). Used for selected accent terminology and structural limitations; §§4–6 provide further reading on compounds, predicates, and affixes. The earlier Keio PDF URL returned 404; the author's current hosting worked. Examples うまい → うまさ and 食べる → 食べたら are located in Table 5 and (29e).

6. **Oshima, David Y. 2014.** “On the Morphological Status of -te, -ta, and Related Forms in Japanese: Evidence from Accent Placement.” *Journal of East Asian Linguistics* 23: 233–265. [Author-hosted manuscript](https://www.hum.nagoya-u.ac.jp/~oshima/docs/tetataratari.pdf). Read especially §§2 and 4 for morphology and competing structural accounts. Manuscript pagination differs from the journal: use section/table locators, not PDF page numbers as journal pagination. This is an argued analysis, not an instruction to adopt its theory unchanged.

7. **Hasegawa, Yoko. 2014.** “Word Structure.” Chapter 6 of *Japanese: A Linguistic Introduction*, 75–88. Cambridge University Press. [Publisher chapter and summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/japanese/word-structure/27CCD8A3730F21D4C1BFC36CCD162070), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139507127.009). Publisher summary and metadata consulted for the organization of conjugation categories and auxiliary constructions; full paid chapter not consulted.

8. **Kobayashi, Megumi. 2003.** “An Examination of Adjective Accent Change in Tokyo Japanese and Factors Influencing the Change” (東京語における形容詞アクセントの変化とその要因). *Journal of the Phonetic Society of Japan* 7(2): 101–113. [DOI / publisher record](https://doi.org/10.24467/onseikenkyu.7.2_101). Metadata and abstract consulted. Supports the need to represent adjective variation and grammatical/phonetic conditioning; it does not establish the individual OJAD values used here.

9. **Warner, Natasha, and Takayuki Arai. 2001.** “Japanese Mora-Timing: A Review.” *Phonetica* 58(1–2): 1–25. [PubMed record and abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11096367/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1159/000028486). Abstract consulted for the empirical qualifications on “mora-timed.” No claim of exact mora isochrony is made.

10. **Youngberg, Connor. 2018.** “The Japanese Moraic Nasal Revisited: A First Glance.” *SOAS Working Papers in Linguistics* 19: 93–116. [SOAS-hosted PDF](https://www.soas.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2023-09/The%20Japanese%20Moraic%20Nasal%20Revisited%20-%20A%20First%20Glance.pdf). Introduction and §2 consulted for special morae and context-dependent nasal realization. Its proposed phonological representation is identified as an analysis, not an uncontested fact.

11. **Venditti, Jennifer J. 1995/1997.** “Japanese ToBI Labelling Guidelines.” First distributed October 1995; reprinted in Kim Ainsworth-Darnell and Mariapaola D'Imperio (eds.), *Papers from the Linguistics Laboratory*, *Ohio State University Working Papers in Linguistics* 50 (1997): 127–162. [Author publication list](https://www1.cs.columbia.edu/~jjv/jjv_publ.html), [OSU volume archive](https://linguistics.osu.edu/research/pubs/papers/archive), [ERIC-hosted volume](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED409733.pdf). Consulted for lexical versus tonal annotation, boundaries, and F0 interpretation. The Columbia direct PDF was too large for the web extractor; the alternate volume provides a traceable copy. MoraMaro does not claim to implement J_ToBI.

12. **Kubozono, Haruo. 1992.** “Modeling Syntactic Effects on Downstep in Japanese.” In *Gesture, Segment, Prosody*, 368–397. Cambridge University Press. [Publisher chapter/summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/gesture-segment-prosody/modeling-syntactic-effects-on-downstep-in-japanese/3D345B4D7EE4999479654F630F991C6D), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511519918.016). Publisher summary consulted for phrase-register downstep and its structural conditioning, rather than treating all downward trends as one phenomenon.
